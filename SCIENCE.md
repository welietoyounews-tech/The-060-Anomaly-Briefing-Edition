# Scientific notes and historical boundaries

## Training-edition interface

The training edition preserves the scientific functions in `models.js` but automates technical instrument settings. New assays use zero alignment offset and a 16-second equivalent integration. Calibration is a guided automatic setup action. Isotope graphs normalize counts automatically. Geology fixes grade at 35%, characteristic lens size at 6 m, compact geometry and no artificial enrichment; the learner varies only age (0 or 2 Ga) and water (0 or 0.65). Dynamics starts with water 0.65 and compares refill 0.035 against zero. Runs stop and save automatically after the required behavior is observed. Advanced function parameters remain inspectable in source but are not player controls.

Isotope matching accepts RMS mismatch below 1.5 percentage points for both elements. The optional xenon exercise fixes retention at 0.8 and accepts relative RMS mismatch below 6%; the learner varies only on/off durations. These tolerances are pedagogical, not experimental uncertainty.

The remaining sections document the scientific engine; references to variable settings describe the available model functions, not additional required training tasks.

**Contains spoilers.** The game is an investigation, not a transcription of the historical experimental sequence. Sample names, records, instrument settings and all assay values are simulated. It represents several independent lines of reasoning in a short playable form.

## Historical grounding

The initial 1972 refinery discrepancy was about 0.7171% U-235 instead of 0.7202%, as recounted by CEA analyst Jean-François Dozol in the IN2P3-hosted contribution below. Further sampling traced depletion to Oklo ore. The game uses 0.720% as a rounded reference and a representative 0.600% shipment to make an instrument exercise legible. Actual ore varied considerably; 0.600% is not claimed as the exact first discovery measurement.

Uranium depletion and trace-element/noble-gas isotope patterns supported ancient fission. At roughly two billion years before present, natural uranium had more U-235 because of its shorter half-life. Uranium-rich geometry and groundwater moderation could support a chain reaction. The geological feasibility model illustrates these requirements without establishing the criticality of a specific deposit.

The 2004 result is distinct from the 1972 discovery. Meshik, Hohenberg and Pravdivtseva studied fission noble gases associated with the natural reactor, finding isotope evidence consistent with approximately 30-minute active periods separated by 2.5 hours of dormancy. The game does not attribute this timing to the original discoverers or to every reactor zone.

Sources:

1. Dozol, “From routine sample measurements in CEA to the Oklo phenomenon,” IN2P3 conference contribution (2022): https://indico.in2p3.fr/event/19295/contributions/111854/
2. USGS, “Gamma-ray spectrometer measurement of 238U/235U in uranium ore from a natural reactor at Oklo, Gabon” (1976): https://www.usgs.gov/publications/gamma-ray-spectrometer-measurement-238u235u-uranium-ore-a-natural-reactor-oklo-gabon
3. Meshik, A. P., Hohenberg, C. M. and Pravdivtseva, O. V. (2004), *Physical Review Letters* 93, 182302. https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.93.182302

## Mass spectrometry

Only U-235 and U-238 are represented; trace U-234 is omitted. Percentages are two-isotope normalized. Sample truth: reference 0.720%, shipment 0.600%, sealed core 0.612%, adjacent ore 0.719% U-235. These are synthetic values.

A measurement is truth + 0.045 × alignment offset + noise. Noise is the centered sum of four independent uniform variates, scaled to standard deviation 0.012 / sqrt(integration seconds) percentage points. This approximates counting-noise reduction with longer integration without claiming an exact Poisson detector model. Displayed ±2σ uncertainty excludes calibration bias.

Calibration requires the reference and peak alignment within ±0.06. It stores a centered correction and sets the offset to zero. Changing alignment invalidates that correction. Uncalibrated measurements remain recorded but are excluded from evidence gates. Spectrum peaks use Gaussian shapes and a logarithmic signal axis so the small U-235 peak remains visible; the spectrum is not a full mass-spectrometer ion-optics simulation.

## Trace-element distributions

Mass bins:

- Nd: 142, 143, 144, 145, 146, 148, 150.
- Ru: 96, 98, 99, 100, 101, 102, 104.

Natural reference arrays are rounded approximate terrestrial isotope abundances. The arrays named `fission` are synthetic teaching signatures, not evaluated fission yields. A sample is generated by mixing 28% natural distribution and 72% synthetic fission distribution, then adding small detector-count noise. Arrays normalize to 100%. The game asks the player to compare both elements, normalize counts and estimate a mixture fraction. A low RMS residual for both supports the fission interpretation in this teaching dataset. It does not imply 72% of uranium underwent fission. Real signatures involve yield chains, neutron capture, migration, and other geochemical effects.

## Ancient isotope abundance

For unconsumed natural uranium, isotope odds evolve backward as

r(t) = r(0) × exp[ln(2) × t × (1/T235 − 1/T238)]

and abundance = 100 × r/(1+r). Here r(0)=0.720/99.280, time is in Ga, T235=0.7038 Ga and T238=4.468 Ga. At 2 Ga this returns about 3.67%. This is not a reconstruction of the already-burned sample. No artificial enrichment is required for accepted natural-condition trials; a separate multiplier allows counterfactual tests.

## Reduced neutron multiplication

`multiplication` combines dimensionless factors:

- eta = 2.43 × 585 × x / [683 × x + 2.68 × (1−x)], for U-235 atom fraction x. These are approximate thermal cross sections in barns and neutrons per fission, not energy-dependent group constants.
- moderation = 1 − exp(−6w).
- utilization = g/(g+6+7w).
- resonance escape = 0.82 + 0.14 × moderation.
- non-leakage = 1/[1 + (2.4/(L × shape))²].
- dilution = 1/[1 + 0.04 × (w/0.8)³].

w is a schematic pore-water saturation, g is ore-grade percentage, and L is characteristic lens size in meters. The leakage functional form is diffusion-inspired. Coefficients are pedagogical, chosen to demonstrate how moderation, parasitic absorption, geometry and age compete. They are not fitted to Oklo transport data. Numerical k values are illustrative, not estimates of actual reactor criticality or design specifications.

An accepted geological conclusion requires three recorded, matched trials: wet ancient natural ore with k>1; the same geometry/grade/water today with k<1; and the same ancient setup dry with k<1. This is a causal comparison rather than a single target-setting puzzle.

## Coupled dynamic model

State: normalized power p, temperature T, water w, venting state and elapsed model time. Explicit Euler step dt=0.15 model minutes:

- dp/dt = 0.8 × (k(w)−1) × p + 0.0008, bounded to [0.001,3]. The small source seeds activity; this is not prompt-neutron point kinetics.
- dw/dt = refill × max(0, initial saturation−w) when not venting; −0.075w when venting.
- dT/dt = 7p − 0.032(T−25) − 100 × boiling rate.

Venting switches on above 99°C and switches off below 68°C. These phenomenological hysteresis thresholds stand in for finite cooling and groundwater return. Temperature is bounded to [25,130]°C, and saturation to [0,1]. Those bounds and the power cap are numerical/model simplifications, not a proof against runaway in real geology. No claim is made that these coefficients reproduce historical cycle periods. There is no fuel burnup, long-term geological evolution or pressure-dependent phase model.

Feedback is a result of coupling, not a prerecorded sine wave. With the default ancient geometry, replenishment produces repeated heating episodes; disabling replenishment leaves depleted water and fading activity. The feedback evidence gate requires a recorded cycling case and a long no-refill control, then the player's interpretation.

## 2004 conceptual xenon exercise

This is explicitly **not a quantitative fit to real xenon measurements**. Three invented effective precursor half-lives (10, 40, 180 minutes) define independent teaching channels. Production is constant during operation. For a precursor with lambda=ln(2)/half-life and retention R, the retained daughter fraction per precursor produced is

R × [1−exp(−lambda × on)]/(lambda × on) × [1−exp(−lambda × off)].

This follows single-precursor production and decay during an active interval, followed by decay in a dormant retention window. Xenon released during the active interval is assumed lost. Targets are synthetic, generated at on=30, off=150 minutes, R=0.8, intentionally illustrating the published approximate reconstruction. Turning decay off suppresses proxy production. The fit criterion is relative RMS error <6% in the training edition, with no exact-phrase or exact-setting requirement.

Real xenon evidence involves multiple nuclide chains, isotope-specific yields, transport, mineral retention and many operating cycles. Three proxy numbers do not recreate that analysis or independently establish the published result.

## Investigation and report gates

Six guided lessons require: calibrated reference and shipment measurements; repeat checks and independent ore/records; matching both trace-element fingerprints; three age/water comparison trials; two completed groundwater simulations; and five evidence-supported report statements. Interpretations can be revised without penalty. Evidence citations are attached automatically. Hypothesis notes and links are optional; the 2004 bonus unlocks after the main report. No report claim is accepted without its collected evidence card.
